Field notes

What the work taught me.

Three practical lessons from building and operating these systems, plus my latest long-form write-ups.

NOTE 01

Human approval is a workflow state, not a checkbox.

If a risky action pauses for review, the request, policy decision and reviewer outcome must remain traceable. That is why the control-plane reference treats approval as explicit state and preserves an audit event before execution.

Design consequenceNothing executes while approval is ambiguous.
NOTE 02

The 126s → 4s improvement did not come from a larger model.

The useful work was in the data path: query shape, indexes, connection pooling and concurrency behavior. Optimizing the expensive path first created a larger gain than changing the intelligence layer.

Design consequenceMeasure the bottleneck before choosing the fix.
NOTE 03

A model release needs a clear reason, not just a green button.

Accuracy, p95 latency, error rate and drift are evaluated together. When a release is blocked, the platform returns the violated policies so a team can act on evidence instead of guessing.

Design consequencePromotion gates stay explainable and reversible.

Questions

Before we talk.

What kind of projects do you take on?

Production AI agents, RAG assistants, LLM automation and MLOps on AWS and Azure. Usually a prototype that works but needs architecture, evaluation, safety controls or lower running cost before real users depend on it.

Can you take on a larger project with a team?

Yes. For larger builds I bring in a small team of engineers I work with, and I stay your single technical lead and point of contact: architecture, delivery plan, code review and handoff. We size the team to the scope, starting with an architecture review or a paid first milestone.

Do you work with teams outside Pakistan?

Yes. I work remotely with teams worldwide from UTC+5, with overlap for European working hours and US mornings, and I am open to relocation for the right full-time role.

How does an engagement usually start?

With a small, paid first milestone: an architecture and risk review, or one production vertical slice. You see working evidence before committing to more.

Which models and tools do you use?

OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, Claude and open models such as Llama through Ollama; LangGraph and LangChain for orchestration; MCP for tool integrations; Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis, Docker and Kubernetes; AWS and Azure for deployment.

How do you keep AI answers reliable?

Every system ships with an evaluation set, source traceability where it matters, groundedness checks, PII redaction, and cost and latency monitoring. When the evidence is not there, the system says so instead of guessing.

Can I hire you full-time?

Yes. I am open to AI/ML engineering roles on-site, hybrid or remote, and to relocation. Reach me by email or on LinkedIn.
